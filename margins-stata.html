A Powerful Post-Estimation Tool in Stata: Margins

Yihan Wang, PhD

2023-04-05

When to Use the margins Command?

For more complex models in Stata, it is often easier to interpret margins — specifically, margins of responses or margins of changes in responses.

Margins are statistics calculated from predictions of a previously fit model at fixed values of some covariates and averaging over the remaining covariates.

Margins answers: “What does my model have to say about such-and-such a group or such-and-such a person?”

Types of Margins of Responses

Abbreviation Name Description
APM Average Predictive Margins Average of responses among actual people in the data
PMM Predictive Margins at the Means Expected response for a person with average characteristics
PMR Predictive Margins at Representative Values Expected response across a range of covariate values

Types of Margins of Changes in Responses

Abbreviation Name Description
AME Average Marginal Effects Average of changes in responses among actual people
MEM Marginal Effects at the Means Expected change for a person with average characteristics
MER Marginal Effects at Representative Values Expected change across a range of covariate values

Where to Use the margins Command?

  • The margins command can only be used after running a regression — it acts on the most recent regression results

  • The marginsplot command graphs any margins or comparisons of margins — it acts on the most recent margins results

regress y x1 x2 x3
margins
marginsplot

Example Data: NHANES II

Margins of Responses Example

Predicting continuous systolic blood pressure (bpsystol) as a function of:

  • Age (age)
  • Diabetes status (diabetes)
  • Geographic region (region)

Using OLS regression

Dataset: NHANES II (Second National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, mid-to-late 1970s) wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/nhanes2

Average Predictive Margins (APM)

The mean predicted value of systolic blood pressure is 130.89, based on each observation in the data.

regress bpsystol age i.diabetes i.region
margins

APM gives the average of the predicted values across all actual observations.

APM: Dummy & Categorical Variables

margins diabetes
margins region
Group Mean Predicted SBP
No diabetes 130.51
Northeast region 131.28
West region 130.47

Predictive Margins at the Means (PMM)

margins, atmeans
margins diabetes, atmeans

The predicted systolic blood pressure for a person with diabetes is 138.31, holding all other covariates at their mean values.

PMM answers: “What is the predicted outcome for a person with perfectly average characteristics?”

Predictive Margins at Representative Values (PMR)

margins, at(age=80)
margins, at(age=(20 30 40 50 60 70 80))
  • Mean predicted SBP with age set to 80 is 151.52
  • Mean predicted SBP across age range 20–80 ranges from 132–152

Example: Margins of Changes in Responses

Predicting probability of diabetes (diabetes) as a function of:

  • Race (black)
  • Sex (female)
  • Age (age)

Using logistic regression with the same NHANES II dataset

logit diabetes i.black i.female age
margins

Average Marginal Effects (AME)

margins, dydx(*)
Group Avg Predicted Probability of Diabetes
Black 8.44%
Non-Black 4.43%
Female 5.15%
Male 4.47%

The average marginal effect of sex: +0.68 percentage points (female vs. male)

Marginal Effects at the Means (MEM)

margins, dydx(*) atmeans
  • Calculate marginal effects with all other covariates fixed at their sample means
  • Gives the predicted probability for each group, holding other variables constant
  • Gives the change in probability when group membership changes from a to b, holding other variables at the mean

Marginal Effects at Representative Values (MER)

margins, dydx(*) at(age=70 black=1)

For a 70-year-old Black person, their predicted probability of diabetes is 19.12%, holding all other variables at their original values.

MER: Across a Range

margins black female, at(age=(20 30 40 50 60 70))
marginsplot
Group Age 20 Age 70
Black 0.61% 8.78%
Female 0.10% 1.53%

Margins with Interactions

Research question: Does marital status (marital) affect the relationship between sex and general happiness (happy), controlling for income (rincome) and education (educ)?

mlogit happy i.sex##i.marital rincome educ
margins marital, at(sex=(0 1))
marginsplot

Dataset: General Social Survey (GSS) — gss.norc.org

⚠️ Interpretation of interactions in multinomial logit models can be tricky — use margins to make results interpretable.

Margins with Interactions: Results

Fixing income and education at specific levels:

  • Different marital statuses have different predicted probabilities of happiness compared to the base category
  • marginsplot visualizes these predicted probabilities across groups

Example: Margins with Dummy Variables

Research question: Does depression affect the relationship between serious illness and 90-day Medicare expenditure post-surgery?

glm tot_paid_by_mc_p90 i.ind_depression##i.ind_si_any, family(gamma) link(log)
margins ind_depression#ind_si_any

Project work using Medpar data linked to HRS data

⚠️ Default regression output coefficients do not directly translate to dollar amounts — use margins to get interpretable results

Margins with Dummy Variables: Results

margins ind_depression#ind_si_any
marginsplot, recast(bar) by(ind_si_any)
Group Avg Predicted 90-day Medicare Expenditure
No depression, no serious illness $6,401.18
Depression, no serious illness $8,611.21
